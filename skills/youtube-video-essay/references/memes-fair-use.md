# Memes, movies, famous art and faces — using them well and safely

Steven wants memes, film moments and well-known art in the videos. This is the playbook: how to
stage them in the house look, and how to keep the channel safe. **Not legal advice** — it is a
risk practice built from US fair-use law and YouTube's rules (researched Nov 2026; sources at the
bottom). When a use is high-stakes (a whole video about one film, anything commercial), ask a lawyer.

## The one question
**"Am I saying something about this thing, or just decorating with it?"**
- Talking *about* it — reading The Matrix as a teaching on emptiness, using "This is fine" to name
  how we deny suffering — is commentary. That is the strongest ground fair use has.
- Using it as wallpaper or for a cheap laugh with no point attached is the weakest. Since the 2023
  Warhol ruling, a new look or mood alone is not enough; the *purpose* has to differ from the original's.

There is no safe number of seconds. Judges weigh four things: why you used it (commentary beats
decoration), what it is, how much and whether you took the heart of it, and whether your video
could replace the original for viewers.

## Four separate systems (do not mix them up)
| System | What it is | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Copyright / fair use | the law | follow the staging rules below; keep a rights log |
| Content ID | YouTube's robot matcher. It has no idea what fair use is and ignores clip length. Film **music** is claimed most. | a claim is not a strike: the owner takes that video's ad money or blocks it. Trim, mute, or dispute with a written rationale |
| Reused-content policy | a monetisation rule for the whole channel, separate from copyright | every borrowed second must sit inside Steven's own commentary; never post bare clips, especially as Shorts |
| Likeness / AI rules | realistic AI of a real person must be disclosed, and the person can still have it removed | never synthesise a real person's face or voice |

## Traffic lights
**Green — use freely**
- Public-domain art: anything published before 1931 (in 2026; the line moves up one year each
  January) and the old masters. Photos of flat public-domain paintings add no new copyright in the US.
  Hokusai, Hiroshige, Kuniyoshi, Hakuin, Sengai, Sesshū, Bosch, Bruegel, Dürer, Blake, Goya, Friedrich,
  Van Gogh, Klimt, Munch's *Scream*, medieval manuscripts, old thangkas and Gandhara sculpture.
- Steven's own footage and photos; AI images Steven made; CC0 material.
- A meme **format rebuilt** with our own or public-domain picture (the joke's structure is an idea,
  not the photo).

**Yellow — only inside commentary, staged by the rules below**
- Film and TV stills and short clips. Meme images that are someone's photo or character.
- Modern art still in copyright (Magritte, Dalí, Escher, Hopper, Rothko, Warhol, Kusama).
- Screenshots of posts, book covers, album covers, game footage.

**Red — do not use**
- Any commercial music, including a film's score under a clip. A single line of dialogue is the
  most we ever keep.
- Whole scenes, endings and twists; sports; music videos.
- Agency photos (Getty, AP, Reuters) — they bill meme users too.
- A meme, character or celebrity face in a **thumbnail**, a sponsor segment, merch or an ad
  (commercial use is where meme owners win: Grumpy Cat's owners won $710k; Pepe's creator sues).
- Realistic AI of real people. Memes tied to hate. Mocking private people from viral clips.

## Staging rules for yellow material
1. **Only what the point needs**: stills 2–4 s; clips usually 3–8 s; all borrowed footage together
   under about 10% of the video.
2. **Picture only**: mute the clip and let Steven talk over it. If the spoken line is the point
   ("There is no spoon"), keep that line alone, ≤ 10 s, subtitled.
3. **Always inside a frame, never full-bleed**: the film frame card or a reprint (below), so it is
   visibly quoted, not re-broadcast.
4. **Credit on screen** in microtext: `THE MATRIX · 1999 · DIR. THE WACHOWSKIS · WARNER BROS.`
   It is honest, it supports the fair-use case, and it matches the print look.
5. **Comment while it is on screen**: voice or type must be about the thing at that moment.
6. **Not the best bit, not the ending**, and nothing borrowed in the first 30 s of the video.
7. **Each source once or twice**, unless the whole video is an analysis of it.

## How to stage them in the house look
| Pattern | What it is | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Reprint** | the still or meme as a one-ink halftone on a flat chapter colour, with the giant word | the default for memes and faces; turns any source into our look |
| **Film frame card** | clip in a rounded window with film-edge marks, credit microtext under it, our line beside it | film clips and stills |
| **Meme rebuild** | same joke structure, our own image: thangka figures, public-domain painting, Steven, AI art | the safest and usually funniest version |
| **Classical art meme** | public-domain painting + deadpan modern caption in heavy sans | green-light humour, any length |
| **Annotated still** | still with hand-drawn circles, arrows and labels | analysis moments ("look at his hands") |
| **Split commentary** | clip in one tile, Steven in the other, reacting or explaining | longer film discussion |
| **Reaction flash** | 12–20 frames of a reprinted face on a hard cut, stepped on twos | one beat of comedy, max three per video |

Tone: memes are seasoning. One to four per video, each earning a point, never during a quote,
a parable or the closing words.

## Workflow
1. **Rights log** per video, `rights.csv`: asset · source and owner · light (green/yellow) ·
   seconds used · the sentence of ours that comments on it. Yellow items without that sentence
   get cut.
2. **Find the source**: Know Your Meme for a meme's origin and owner; prefer the earliest clean
   copy; never pull from agency sites.
3. **Stage** with the patterns above (`MediaCard`, `Halftone` in `src/design`).
4. **Check before publishing**: upload unlisted, wait for Studio's "Checks". If a claim appears:
   first trim, mute or swap the asset; dispute only when the use clearly follows every rule above.
   A lost dispute that escalates can become a strike, so do not dispute weak cases.
5. **Description**: list film and art credits under "Sources".

Dispute text (edit the brackets): "This video is original commentary on Buddhist philosophy. It
quotes [N seconds, picture only] of [title, year] at [timestamp] in order to analyse [the idea].
My narration discusses the clip while it is shown, the clip is a small part of the work and of my
video, and the video does not substitute for the original."
