## Midjourney makes the picture; Higgsfield adds only motion

The single idea that makes image-to-video work is a division of labour. The still already contains the subject, style, composition and light. The video prompt should therefore describe **only what changes**: one slow action, one camera move, a little ambient motion. Guides for Kling say repeating what the image shows "wastes prompt space", and Higgsfield's own guidance orders a prompt as subject and action first, then camera, with a line on what the camera must not do ([Atlas Cloud Kling guide](https://www.atlascloud.ai/it/blog/guides/kling-ai-text-to-video); [Higgsfield Seedance 2.5 prompting guide](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide), both [WEB]). No end-to-end Midjourney-to-Higgsfield tutorial was found; the pipeline below is stitched from guides to each half.

### The pipeline, step by step

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pick the shape first | 9:16 for Shorts, Reels and TikTok; 16:9 for the website and long-form. Generate in Midjourney with the matching `--ar` | Kling 3.0 accepts only 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1 [LIVE]; a mismatched still gets cropped or stretched |
| 2. Lock the look | Choose one hero image and reuse it as `--sref` on every still in a series | one style reference is how a series stays visually consistent |
| 3. Compose for motion | One main subject, space above and at the sides, hands apart, no small text | camera moves crop inward; fine text and overlapping hands morph |
| 4. Upscale and download the original | Upscale in Midjourney, download the full-size file from the web app | soft or compressed sources produce shimmering video |
| 5. Repair before animating | In Higgsfield: outpaint to the right ratio, upscale to 2K, or edit out stray details | fixing a still costs 2 credits; re-rolling video costs many more |
| 6. Upload as the start frame | Put the image in the start-frame slot of Kling 3.0, not a reference slot | a start frame fixes the first frame's pixels; a reference lets the model redraw your art |
| 7. Draft | 5 seconds, std, sound off, one variant, motion-only prompt | 6.25 credits buys the information you need |
| 8. Change one thing per retry | Wording or camera or duration, never all three | each credit then tells you something |
| 9. Final | Re-run the winning prompt at pro or on Kling 4.0; upscale afterwards if needed | a re-run is a new sample, not an upscale of the draft |

### The motion prompt template

A workable template, synthesised from the cited rules **[UNTESTED]**:

```text
[Subject] [one slow action, or "does not move"]. [One or two ambient movers:
smoke, leaves, water, cloth, flame]. Camera: [one move + speed], [what it must
not do]. [Light change, if any]. Keep [face / art style] unchanged.
```

Keep it to roughly 30–100 words with the action in the first sentence. Leave out the robe colour and setting (the image shows them), stacked camera moves, mood adjectives such as "serene" or "epic", and quality spam such as "8k, masterpiece". The live catalogue shows **no negative-prompt field** on Kling 3.0, Seedance or Cinema Studio, so say what must stay still inside the prompt itself. The weak-versus-strong contrast below shows the difference **[UNTESTED]**:

```text
WEAK:   A beautiful serene Buddha meditating under a tree, cinematic, 8k,
        golden light, epic camera movement

STRONG: Leaves above sway and drop two or three at a time. Light through the
        canopy shifts slowly across the figure's shoulder. Camera: slow dolly
        in, ends on a medium close-up. The figure does not move.
```

### One camera move per clip, and the slow ones are the safe ones

Camera control lives in three places: the classic one-click preset library on the website (Higgsfield's page claims 50+ moves, and one guide counted 66 on 17 November 2026), Cinema Studio's settings panel, and the Editing Room, which re-films an existing clip with one of 18 moves or 16 fixed angles ([Higgsfield Camera Controls](https://higgsfield.ai/camera-controls); [aliteq](https://aliteq.com/higgsfield-camera-moves), [WEB]; [Editing Room presets, live read](https://higgsfield.ai/editing-room), [LIVE]). Stacking orbit, zoom and pan in one short clip "often creates warped geometry" ([apidot](https://apidot.ai/blog/kling-3-0-image-to-video-guide), [WEB]), so use one. Higgsfield's own descriptions draw a distinction that matters for flat artwork: "Zoom in" is optical and the camera does not travel, while "Dolly in" travels and forces the model to invent depth. On a painting, a slow push or optical zoom changes the art least.

| Intent | Move | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Draw the viewer inward | Slow push in, dolly in | safest on stylised art |
| Reveal the place, close a scene | Dolly out, crane up | good openers and closers |
| Show the scale of a statue or temple | Crane up, crane down | vertical motion keeps faces stable |
| Presence around a still figure | Orbit left or right, 15–30 degrees | a full 360 forces the model to invent the unseen back |
| Landscape or monastery fly-through | FPV drone | wide establishing images only |
| Avoid for calm content | Barrel roll, robo arm, crash zoom, Dutch angle push | high-energy advertising language |

The selection is the researchers' judgement **[UNTESTED]** from Higgsfield's live move descriptions. Text-only camera lines you can drop into any Kling prompt **[UNTESTED]**: `slow dolly in, ends on a close-up of the hands`; `slow arc right 20 degrees around the seated figure, eye level`; `crane up and tilt down, revealing the courtyard`; `locked-off tripod shot, no camera movement`. Fixed preset moves run 7–15 seconds, so they cost more than a 5-second prompted move; the Editing Room's moves are also described in terms of "the person", and their behaviour on statues or landscapes is untested.

### When a shot goes wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Subject morphs or melts | too much motion, busy or soft still, long clip | one slow action, locked camera, 5 s, sharper and simpler still |
| Statue or painted figure "comes alive" | models add motion nobody asked for | state "the statue does not move"; limit motion to smoke, light, cloth, water |
| Slow vertical bobbing of the subject | reported Kling 3.0 quirk | locked camera, stabilising suffix, re-roll |
| Hands or mudra deform | close-up hands are Kling's weakest area | frame wider, keep hands motionless |
| Painting turns photographic | still was in a reference slot, or prompt uses photo words | start-frame slot; "preserve the illustration style"; drop "35mm", "bokeh" |
| Unwanted cuts | model defaults to coverage | "one continuous shot, no cuts"; multi-shot off |
| Flicker in fine detail | dense texture, low resolution | simplify, then Video Deflicker and upscale |
| Script or mantra text scrambles | fine text plus camera motion | keep text out of the still; add titles in the edit |
| Seedance rejects the upload | its filter blocks realistic human faces | use Kling, or a stylised still |

Causes and fixes come from third-party guides and vendor notes **[WEB]**: Kling's hand and group-face weaknesses ([Genra](https://genra.ai/blog/kling-3-0-complete-guide)), the bobbing observation ([Kittl](https://www.kittl.com/blogs/kittl-review-kling-3-0-4k/)), warped faces from aggressive camera moves ([glbgpt](https://www.glbgpt.com/resources/kling-3-0-prompt-guide-for-better-ai-videos/)), and Seedance's real-face block ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-404/reference-content-can-t-include-real-human-faces-when-using-seedance-2-0-and-seedance-2-0-fast-1631867)). The statue and mudra rows are inference by analogy; no reviewer documented behaviour on statues, religious iconography or paintings, and no Reddit or YouTube hands-on material could be reached, so community experience is missing from this whole section.

### Keeping a look and a character across a series

Higgsfield's much-advertised Soul ID is the wrong tool here. It is a trained likeness of a real person, it works only inside the Soul model it was trained in, and Higgsfield's help centre says it "is not for inventing a character that does not exist" ([Higgsfield help centre: Soul ID](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-create-and-use-a-soul-id-character), [WEB]). For an illustrated monk or a recurring grove, the right tools are **Elements**: saved character, environment or prop references that you call in a prompt with an @ handle. The live catalogue lists Elements as honoured by Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2.6, Seedream 4.5, Cinema Studio Image 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Cinema Studio Video v2 and 3.0, and by Kling 3.0 only when a start image is also supplied; support on Seedance 2.5, Cinema Studio 4.0 and Kling 4.0 is unconfirmed **[LIVE]** ([Higgsfield catalogue, live read](https://higgsfield.ai/)).

The practical recipe is to make every start frame from the same references and then animate from stills, because a start frame is a stronger lock than any reference. Make one hero still and three or four views of the character in Midjourney, save them as Elements, generate each shot's start frame in one image model, and animate. Higgsfield warns that errors accumulate when you chain clips from last frames: chain two or three, then restart from a fresh still ([Higgsfield: keep AI character consistent](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/keep-ai-character-consistent), [WEB]). A start-frame prompt using Elements **[UNTESTED]**:

```text
@monk seated at the edge of @bodhi_grove at dusk, wide shot from a low angle,
same painterly style and palette as the reference image, soft orange rim
light, open sky in the upper third.
```

For the Buddha specifically, the researchers recommend consistency through iconography rather than a trained "face": choose one traditional form (Gandharan, Sukhothai or thangka style) and hold it. The connector's bundled character-sheet workflow offers 15 styles including Painterly 2D, but its rule is "original characters only", which fits an invented narrator and not a sacred figure **[LIVE]**.

## Loops, long videos and the website need an editor's trick, not more credits

### Seamless loops for page backgrounds

A loop is made by putting **the same still in both the start-frame and end-frame slots** and asking only for motion that naturally repeats. Kling's own guide describes this method and warns that matching frames guarantee the cut, not even pacing in between ([Kling](https://kling.ai/topics/spotify-canvas-maker), [WEB]). In your catalogue, both slots exist on Kling 3.0, Kling 4.0, Veo 3.1 Lite, Wan 2.7 and 3.0, MAGI-2, FLUX 3 Video, all Cinema Studio versions and Seedance; Kling 2.6, Kling 3.0 Turbo and Kling 4.0 Flash take a start frame only **[LIVE]**. There is a real doubt to test: one survey reports several models advertise end frames but ignore them on some hosts, and one Higgsfield page says End Frame was removed from some newer versions ([mer.vin](https://mer.vin/2026/09/which-ai-video-models-actually-take-a-first-and-last-frame/), [WEB]). A loop prompt **[UNTESTED]**:

```text
Gentle cyclical motion only: flame flickers, incense smoke rises and thins,
prayer flags lift and settle. Camera completely static. The final frame
matches the first frame exactly in pose, lighting and framing for a
seamless loop.
```

Choose flames, water, smoke, drifting cloud or slow rotation, and avoid one-way events such as a bird leaving or a petal landing, which cannot be looped by prompting ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/how-to-make-seamless-ai-video-loops), [WEB]). Even with matched frames, AI clips drift, and a case study found the loop still jumped on each restart ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/YgLoLZMR-making-ai-nature-videos-loop-forever), [WEB]). The forgiving fix is to blend the last 10–15% of the clip over the first, which the free tool ffmpeg can do. These commands are the researchers' drafts **[UNTESTED]**; the second line's `-stream_loop` usage is documented ([OTTVerse](https://ottverse.com/how-to-loop-videos-using-ffmpeg-step-by-step-tutorial/), [WEB]):

```bash
# 8 s clip with a 1 s crossfade baked in -> 7 s seamless loop
ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -filter_complex \
 "[0:v]split[a][b];[a]trim=start=1,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS[body];[b]trim=end=1,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS[head];[body][head]xfade=transition=fade:duration=1:offset=6[v]" \
 -map "[v]" -an -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p loop.mp4

# shrink for the website (silent, 720p, fast start)
ffmpeg -i loop.mp4 -an -vf scale=1280:-2 -c:v libx264 -crf 26 -movflags +faststart hero.mp4
```

The `offset` must equal the body length minus the fade length, so adjust it to your real clip. A forward-then-reversed "ping-pong" is the fallback for scenes without directional motion; it fails on smoke and flowing water because they visibly run backwards.

### Embedding in the static site

Browsers block autoplay with sound, and iPhone Safari needs both `muted` and `playsinline`; the standard pattern lists a WebM source before MP4 ([MDN: Autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay); [web.dev: Video performance](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance), [WEB]). A snippet for `index.html`, `meditation.html` or `visualization.html` **[UNTESTED]**:

```html
<video autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"
       poster="img/hero-poster.webp" width="1280" height="720" aria-hidden="true">
  <source src="video/hero.webm" type="video/webm">
  <source src="video/hero.mp4"  type="video/mp4">
</video>
```

The researchers' working targets, which are judgement and not from a source, are a 6–12 second loop at 1280x720 with no audio track and roughly 1–3 MB, a poster image under about 100 KB, `object-fit: cover` in CSS, the poster alone for visitors who set `prefers-reduced-motion`, and a visible pause control. Keep only short silent loops in the repository and host full meditations on YouTube, since large video files bloat a git-hosted site.

### Getting past 30 seconds

No model makes a ten-minute video. Longer pieces are built four ways, and the first is the one to learn. **Assembly**: many 5–10 second clips cut together under narration and music, in the connector's sandbox, the Editing Room timeline (free MP4 export) or any editor. **Repetition**: a baked loop repeated to length without re-encoding, which the researchers sketch as `ffmpeg -stream_loop -1 -i loop.mp4 -t 1800 -c copy bg_30min.mp4` **[UNTESTED]**; because a 7-second loop repeated for an hour is noticeable, make three to six different loops of the same scene and dissolve between them every few minutes. **Start-to-end transitions**: still A as start frame, still B as end frame, and a prompt describing only the change, for example **[UNTESTED]**:

```text
Day turns to night: the sun sinks behind the ridge, shadows lengthen, sky
shifts from gold to deep blue, lanterns light one by one. The seated figure
does not move. Camera locked.
```

**Native extension**: Seedance 2.5 and Cinema Studio 4.0 can extend an existing clip forwards or backwards, and Kling 4.0, Wan 3.0 and MAGI-2 reach 30 seconds in one generation **[LIVE]**; whether extension keeps 1080p and how the website's buttons expose it were not verified.

The connector also ships a ready-made "faceless-video" workflow with a "Fairy Tale & Myth" storybook type, a locked narrator voice and burned subtitles, which sounds ideal for Jataka tales **[LIVE]**. Its default rule fights your tone, though: every 10-second block is five hard cuts of about 2 seconds, with "no shot longer than 2.5s". Its alternative "narrated stills" mode (one held image per spoken beat) or a hand-built shot list suits contemplative pacing better. The researchers estimate a 2–3 minute run at 240–360 credits for clips alone (12–18 blocks at 20 credits), a figure the workflow itself does not state.

### Sound, added once at the end

Generate visuals silent and add narration, music and effects in the edit; native audio is regenerated on every re-roll and will not match from clip to clip. Audio is cheap: about 0.9 credits for a 275-character narration line on Text to Speech V2 (ElevenLabs engine), 8.72 credits per minute of ElevenLabs Music v2.5 (26.16 for three minutes, maximum ten minutes), and 3.84 for a minute on the older v2 **[LIVE]** ([Higgsfield audio estimator, live quotes](https://higgsfield.ai/pricing)). The connector can also search an Epidemic Sound effects library, and a live search for "Tibetan singing bowl" returned real recordings from 16 to 117 seconds long; whether using them costs credits and what licence covers YouTube were not stated. A music prompt **[UNTESTED]**:

```text
slow ambient meditation bed, bansuri flute and tanpura drone, sparse
singing-bowl accents, no percussion, calm and spacious
```

The researchers advise against generating Pali, Sanskrit or Tibetan chanting with AI, since it tends to produce gibberish and is the audio equivalent of a fake quote. Preset voices carry only a name and gender, so audition them by ear, or record your own voice, which is also the strongest defence against YouTube's "inauthentic content" test.

## Five recipes fit inside one month of 970 credits

All five budgets use live quotes multiplied out by the researchers and this report **[UNTESTED arithmetic on LIVE prices]**. They assume Midjourney supplies the stills, three takes per kept loop and two to three per kept shot, and that image-to-video with an end frame costs the same as the quoted settings, which was not separately confirmed. Ask for a quote before each batch.

**Recipe 0, calibration (about 20–40 credits).** Before any production, run three Kling 3.0 std silent 5-second tests on one statue still: start frame only; the same image as start and end frame; and the "statue stays completely still" prompt with a slow push-in. This answers the three biggest unknowns in the research for 18.75 credits: whether the end frame is honoured on your account, whether statues "come alive", and whether a loop needs the ffmpeg crossfade. Repeat once on a painted still.

**Recipe 1, five looping page backgrounds (about 115 credits).** One still each for `index.html`, `meditation.html`, `visualization.html`, `guided-meditations.html` and `buddhas.html`; Kling 3.0 pro, sound off, 5 seconds, same image as start and end frame; three takes each at 7.5 credits; bake the crossfade and compress with ffmpeg.

**Recipe 2, a sourced-quote short (about 61 credits).** A 15–30 second 9:16 video: one verified verse on screen with its reference, one sentence of your own commentary, three different slow shots. Three shots times three takes at 6.25 credits is 56.25; narration about 0.9; a one-minute music bed on ElevenLabs Music v2 3.84. Swapping one final for a Kling 4.0 1080p hero shot adds 18.75.

**Recipe 3, a three-minute narrated story (about 355 credits).** For example Kisa Gotami and the mustard seed, in one storybook style, 16:9. Twenty-four 5-second shots at two takes each on Kling 3.0 std silent is 300 credits; ten still repairs at 2 credits is 20; a 450-word narration is about 9 (extrapolated linearly from one quote, unverified); three minutes of music is 26.16. Each clip is held or slowed in the edit so that 24 clips cover three minutes with stills between.

**Recipe 4, a ten-minute guided meditation (about 180–200 credits).** Four different loops of one scene at three takes each on Kling 3.0 pro is 90 credits; a ten-minute music bed on v2.5 is about 87 (extrapolated from the per-minute rate); narration is free if you record it yourself or about 20 with text-to-speech. Assemble by repetition and slow dissolves.

## Monetisation, disclosure and respect decide what is worth making

### What Higgsfield and the platforms allow

Higgsfield's help centre, as seen in search summaries, says the company claims no ownership of your inputs or outputs and does not restrict commercial use, naming YouTube monetisation explicitly; paid-plan output is watermark-free; you remain responsible for third-party rights; and legal indemnity is Enterprise-only ([Higgsfield help centre: ownership](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/account-and-privacy/who-owns-my-generations-and-can-i-use-them-commercially), [WEB]). Nobody read the Terms of Use itself. A third-party blog says the terms also license your content for model training, which sits in tension with the help centre's "limited to operating the service" wording, and the terms' effective date appears as both 27 August and 23 October 2026 ([MindStudio](https://mindstudio.ai/blog/higgsfield-terms-of-service-backlash), [WEB]). Moderation is applied mostly by each underlying model, so a prompt refused by one model can pass on another; nothing found addresses religious figures either way, and this could not be tested without generating ([Higgsfield help centre: flagged content](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/troubleshooting/content-flagged-as-nsfw), [WEB]).

YouTube's rules bite harder than Higgsfield's. A clarification effective 16 July 2026 makes "inauthentic content" non-monetisable, covering templated or near-identical videos whether AI-made or not, content lacking a clear narrative arc, and AI personas presenting health, legal or financial information; AI used as a production tool remains acceptable when videos add original value ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/), [WEB]). A feed of identical quote-over-statue clips is the textbook target, so each video needs its own script, a real explanation or story, a cited source and varied shots. The third category is a reason never to let a synthetic monk make health claims for meditation.

Master every short at 1080x1920 and keep captions and citations inside a central 900x1400 area so platform buttons do not cover them ([BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/social-video-specs-2026), [WEB]). In practice, a photoreal monk or temple gets labelled everywhere, while ink wash, thangka-style and mandala animation fall outside YouTube's requirement; the researchers suggest labelling anyway, which costs nothing and suits a channel whose subject includes right speech. On money, vendor blogs put faceless Shorts at roughly $0.01–$0.50 per 1,000 views, so shorts are for discovery and for sending people to the site; no Buddhist-specific revenue or retention data was found, and the suggested video lengths in this guide are judgement ([ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/faceless-youtube-earnings-2026/), [WEB]). Music and voice licensing is a real gap: third-party summaries say commercial rights for AI music depend on the paid tier at the time of creation and that a licence does not prevent YouTube claims, but Higgsfield's own audio licensing terms were not researched ([InVideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-music-copyright-comparison/), [WEB]). Keep a simple log of tool, plan and date for every track.

### Depicting the Buddha and monks

The cautionary tale is "Yang Mun", an AI-generated monk persona with millions of followers whose advice three experts said was "not based on any Buddhist scripture", whose site claimed he was a real person, and whose creator reportedly earned more than $300,000; Venerable Juewei called the use of a monk's appearance "completely inappropriate" ([Columbia News Service](https://columbianewsservice.com/2026/03/24/millions-found-comfort-in-a-buddhist-monk-but-he-was-never-real/), [WEB]). The strictest published guidance comes from Thailand's Knowing Buddha Organization, which objects to Buddha images used "as if they were furniture or decorative objects" or as merchandise and has a specific statement against AI caricature of the Buddha and monks; it is one organisation's view, and some of its positions go beyond common practice ([Knowing Buddha: dos and don'ts](https://knowingbuddha.org/dos-and-donts); [The World](https://theworld.org/stories/2021/03/09/thai-organization-s-crusade-against-blaspheming-buddha), [WEB]). Thai authorities asked police to act over AI images of monks in staged scenes in 2023 ([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2023/11/thai-buddhist-office-cyber-police-arrest-ai-images), [WEB]). A statue retailer's blog adds the practical point that AI often confuses Buddha and bodhisattva features and gets mudras wrong ([Butuzou](https://butuzou.com/blogs/blog/can-ai-generated-buddhist-deity-images-be-respectful), [WEB]).

From these the researchers derive working rules for this project, which are their inference and not any authority's ruling. Show the Buddha whole, upright, centred and high in the frame, never as a cropped head, on the floor, or as a prop. Do not animate the Buddha talking, lip-syncing, dancing or reacting; let light, smoke and camera move while the figure stays still, which also happens to be what the models do best. No comic, action-hero or meme treatment, and no monks in gag scenes. Keep logos and calls to action off the figure, and on the website keep Buddha imagery out of footers and navigation. Check every still for the correct mudra, five fingers, the ushnisha and elongated ears, and name the tradition an image or story comes from rather than blending them. For a narrator, use your own voice or a clearly illustrated figure, and state plainly that any recurring character is an AI illustration, not a real monastic.

### Quotes that are actually the Buddha's

Misattributed "Buddha quotes" are widespread and documented: the Fake Buddha Quotes project has traced them since 2003 and defines a fake as a quote that cannot be found in a canonical text ([Tricycle](https://tricycle.org/magazine/fake-buddha-quotes/), [WEB]). Thanissaro Bhikkhu's list of things the Buddha never said includes "Life is suffering" and "Everything is impermanent" ([Lion's Roar](https://www.lionsroar.com/what-the-buddha-never-said/), [WEB]). The rule for scripts is simple: every quote carries a canonical reference and a translator credit on screen, paraphrases are labelled as paraphrases, and any unsourced line is searched on fakebuddhaquotes.com first. For monetised video the cleanest source is Bhikkhu Sujato's translations on SuttaCentral, which are dedicated to the public domain; Access to Insight's texts are offered on a no-charge basis and its licence labels for Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translations conflict between mirrors ([SuttaCentral copyright, mirror](https://dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/copyright.html); [Access to Insight FAQ](https://accesstoinsight.org/faq.html), [WEB]).

The researchers offered two starter lists from their own background knowledge, **neither checked in this research**. Passages to verify on SuttaCentral before use: Dhammapada 1–2, 5, 183 and 277–279; SN 36.6 (the two arrows); MN 22 (the raft); AN 3.65 (the Kalama Sutta, often misquoted); Snp 1.8 (the Metta Sutta). Lines commonly listed as fake or doubtful, to confirm individually: "Three things cannot be long hidden: the sun, the moon, and the truth"; "Holding on to anger is like grasping a hot coal"; "Peace comes from within. Do not seek it without"; "The mind is everything. What you think you become"; "You yourself, as much as anybody in the entire universe, deserve your love and affection". The site's existing `quotes.html` should be audited against both before any quote becomes a video.

## Conclusion

The research turns an intimidating 150-model catalogue into a small craft: your Midjourney eye chooses the picture, a 6-credit Kling draft tells you whether it moves well, and an editor's crossfade does what no model can. The quiet surprise is that the constraints line up. The motion that is cheapest and least error-prone (a locked camera, drifting smoke, a figure that does not move) is also the motion that depicts the Buddha most respectfully, and the videos YouTube will pay for (distinct scripts, a real arc, a cited source) are the ones that make an honest teaching site. Scarcity of credits, platform policy and religious etiquette all push toward fewer, stiller, better-sourced pieces.

What remains unknown is unusually cheap to find out. Nearly every gap flagged above, from whether end frames work to whether a statue starts breathing, can be settled with a handful of 5-second tests on your own stills, which no vendor blog can substitute for. Spend the first 40 credits on learning how the models treat your artwork, write down what you see, and the remaining 900 will go much further than any tutorial's prompt library would take them.
